HydroFly uses timflow's transient analytical-element engine in Python. Pumping-induced head changes are calculated at every displayed mesh vertex. Each slider evaluates a new constant-rate scenario from day zero; fly moves are a sequence of candidate designs, not elapsed physical time.

The translucent surface is confined-aquifer piezometric head, not a free water table. The pit is a control footprint, not a hydraulic boundary. Terrain and the fly are illustrative; geology is simplified. The target applies to 25 sampled pit points at the selected pumping duration, following a pre-dewatering period. It does not establish safety everywhere or at earlier times.

Cost = total rate / 60,000 + 10,000 × mean squared normalised target deficit + 0.02 × mean squared normalised excess drawdown. The benchmark optimiser enforces the target at all pit controls. Your fly strategy makes one-well decisions from Python model responses. Each action is evaluated after arrival. No reinforcement learning is used.

Verified against AnaFlow's Theis solution, multi-well superposition and recovery. See the repository's numerical tests and diagnostic plots for scope and tolerances.
